<h2 class="name">NAME</h2>
<p> amstex &#8211; structured text formatting and typesetting </p>
<h2>SYNOPSIS</h2>
<p> <code>amstex</code> [<i>first-line</i>]</p>
<h2>DESCRIPTION</h2>
<p> This manual page is not meant to be exhaustive. The complete documentation for this version of TeX can be found in the info file or manual <i>Web2C: A TeX implementation</i>. </p>
<p> The AmSTeX language is described in the book <i>The Joy of TeX </i>. AmSTeX is a TeX macro package, not a modification to the TeX source program, so all the capabilities described in <code><a href="/docs/linux/man/1-tex/">tex</a></code>(1) are present. </p>
<p> The AmSTeX macros encourage writers to think about the content of their documents, rather than the form. The ideal, not always realized, is to have no formatting commands (like &#8220;switch to italic&#8221; or &#8220;skip 2 picas&#8221;) in the document at all; instead, everything is done by specific markup instructions: &#8220;emphasize&#8221;, &#8220;start a section&#8221;. </p>
<p> For authors more familiar with LaTeX, an AMS-supported package called <i>amsmath.sty</i> is available, among others.</p>
